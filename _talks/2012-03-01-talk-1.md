Over the past two decades, we have entered the platform era, during which the pervasiveness of digital platforms has fundamentally reshaped the interactions of social actors and embedded itself in the daily lives of ordinary people. Against this background, the term ‘platform urbanism’ was proposed as a theoretical framework for researching the unprecedented scale and scope of platform economy actors and their impacts.

Motivated by this emerging research agenda, Weike is conducting his PhD research on platform-driven transformation in rural China. In this seminar, he shared his ongoing research. Specifically, he presented how e-commerce platforms facilitated the expansion of the non-agricultural landscape and changes in local inhabitants’ livelihoods in the rural areas of one of the largest urban agglomerations in the world (the Greater Bay Area of China, largely coinciding with the Pearl River Delta). The investigation draws on cases of e-commerce villages (exemplified by Taobao Village), where clusters of small-scale manufacturing activities heavily rely on the online retail networks of the largest Chinese e-commerce platform, Taobao (also known as Alibaba).
